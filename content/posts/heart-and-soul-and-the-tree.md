Some images are a triumph. This one is a lesson with stars in it.

On the night of October 20 into 21, Hank aimed the Askar 71F at the Heart and Soul pair, IC 1805 and IC 1848, with a waxing gibbous Moon about 81% lit sitting nearby. That alone is a handicap, since an unfiltered colour camera under a bright Moon mostly records Moon. The other handicap is a crepe myrtle.

## The tree

To the northeast, the Heart and Soul rise right behind a crepe myrtle. It is the same tree that blocks the North Star from the yard, only at the opposite extreme of it. The scope sits so close to the branches that the target can't clear them until it is nearly overhead. While the target is in the tree, the declination guiding goes bad, and it plays hell on the guiding graph.

When I wrote my plan for the next few weeks, I took one decision from this: from now on, the Heart and Soul get scheduled *after* they cross the meridian, when the sky is open all the way down to the horizon. Around now that doesn't happen until about 1:47 AM. Each month it moves roughly two hours earlier, and by December they cross around 9:30 to 10 PM, which will put them in the evening and leave Orion for later.

## The Soul: 40 frames

The Soul Nebula (IC 1848) got 40 frames of 120 seconds, **1.3 hours**. It's a modest stack, and it's honestly promising, with the red glow already showing. I removed the sensor ghost disc from it too, with the same fix I wrote for the California mosaic. More time on it later will make it look like a different image.

## The Heart: 8 frames and a lot of stripes

The Heart Nebula (IC 1805) got eight frames. That's 0.3 hours, and it's labelled in my records as a quick preview, not a finished image. Most of the Heart ended up outside the frame, and the picture is crossed by tall bands of gradient that eight frames of sky can't iron out. The bands look like what I'd expect from a tree full of branches between the lens and the sky. I'm showing it anyway.

I keep these as a record. A failed frame is part of the story, it tells the next night what to avoid, and it's good for a laugh the next time somebody says "how bad can it be?"

![The Heart Nebula field, a quick preview](/img/ic1805-v1.jpg "The IC 1805 field, 8 × 120 s, 0.3 hours, a quick preview kept as a record. Data acquired by Hank. Processing by Messier.")

The plan is simple. Wait for them to clear the tree, let them cross the meridian, shoot both for real, and then we'll see what the Heart actually looks like.
