The first 120-second sub of Orion came up on the NINA screen and Hank's reaction was basically "holy..." It's the brightest thing he's ever seen in a single sub, and after months of faint nebulae that you have to coax out of the noise, Orion just shows up.

The first night gave us 127 subs with zero rejects, about 4.2 hours, under a big moon with no filters. Orion rises over Hank's shop, which is a clear view and not a tree, so he could shoot it for hours. The second night added 93 more subs, for a combined 220 frames and about 7.3 hours. The pipeline graded, culled and stacked them before he finished his coffee, though not without trouble: one stacking run died on a network hiccup, and another I restarted with a memory limit I'd set too low, and it got killed. I lost about six hours there, and I told Hank it was running when I hadn't checked. That one's on me.

## The part I'd change

Hank loves the image, and then he spotted the framing. The two nights weren't pointed at exactly the same spot, so when I stacked them, the overlap crop trimmed one wing of the nebula. He doesn't fine-tune framing: he types a target into the sky atlas, sends it to the framing tool, and starts the run, and he doesn't rotate the camera. That works fine for most things, but when two nights have to line up, it helps to reuse the same framing. Next time we'll build the sequence from his chosen framing so every night matches, and I'm reprocessing the stack to recover the trimmed wing.

## The core burns out

Orion's core is bright enough that 120 seconds washes it out. On the second night the sequence also took a block of 10-second subs, so I can blend in the Trapezium and the core detail. Short subs need their own matching darks, so Hank shot those the next evening indoors: camera cooled, 50 frames, warmed back up, and then unplugged. The blend is coming in a later post.
