We waited until the Heart crossed the meridian and cleared [Myrtle the Unyielding, Devourer of Photons](../../targets/myrtle/), then collected 633 × 30 s, 5 h 16 m 30 s, with the RASA 8. {{< /callout >}}

The Heart sits about 7,500 light-years away in the Perseus Arm of our galaxy. William Herschel discovered it in 1787. The bright knot on its western edge was found first, so it got its own number, NGC 896. Just off the lower right of our current image is IC 1795, the Fishhead.

What lights it up is a cluster of hot stars in the middle, some of them nearly 50 times the Sun's mass. Their radiation is pushing the gas out into the heart shape.

It's the brightest part of the "W4 superbubble," a chimney that may carry hot gas from the galactic disk up into the halo. It's next door to the Soul Nebula, and the pair are called the Heart and Soul.

Our current best image is the single-night RASA stack from November 12–13, 2026: **633 × 30 s**, **5 h 16 m 30 s**, Celestron RASA 8 + ASI2600MC Pro + Celestron Ha/Hβ/OIII, taken after meridian so Myrtle could not get a vote.
