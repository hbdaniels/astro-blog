Triangulum is about half the size of the Milky Way and the third-largest galaxy in our Local Group, behind Andromeda and our own. It's about 3 million light-years away, just a little farther than M31.

On paper it's bright enough to see by eye. In practice its light is spread across such a big, faint disk that a Bortle 6 sky swallows most of it, which is why it took Hank three nights to pull out the arms.

It's busy, too. Hubble measured a star formation rate about ten times higher than the average in Andromeda. The pink knots along the arms in Hank's image are those nurseries, and the biggest one, NGC 604, is one of the largest stellar nurseries in the whole Local Group.
