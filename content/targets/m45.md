The Pleiades contain over a thousand stars loosely bound by gravity, but a handful of the brightest ones are what you see with your eyes. It's about 445 light-years away, though NASA notes that number isn't universally agreed on.

Nobody discovered it; people have known it forever. Galileo was the first to look at it through a telescope.

The blue haze is the part Hank's long exposures are for. It's dust the cluster happens to be passing through, lit up by the stars. Near the star Merope, the starlight is pushing the smaller dust grains back harder than the big ones, which sorts the cloud into those faint streaks.
