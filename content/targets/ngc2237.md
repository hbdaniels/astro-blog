The Rosette is a large hydrogen cloud in Monoceros, about 5,000 light-years away. At its center is the open cluster NGC 2244, whose hot young stars are carving a cavity in the gas and lighting the rim that gives the nebula its flower-like shape.

From our backyard it is a winter target: on these November nights it cleared after the Elephant's Trunk, once Myrtle stopped owning the early Heart.

Our current best image is the two-night RASA stack from November 5–6, 2026: **879 × 30 s**, **7 h 19 m 30 s**, Celestron RASA 8 + ASI2600MC Pro + Celestron Ha/Hβ/OIII, camera ghost cropped from the lower right.
