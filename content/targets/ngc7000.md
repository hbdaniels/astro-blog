The North America Nebula is a large hydrogen region in the constellation Cygnus, about 2,200 light-years away. In a correct-parity, nearly north-up image the bright ionization front known as the Cygnus Wall sits where Mexico and Central America would be, and the dark bay next to it stands in for the Gulf of Mexico. East is to the left, so the continent is a mirror of the terrestrial map. It is still unmistakable.

From our backyard the west is clear, which makes this a practical early-evening target on November nights when the Heart is still behind [Myrtle the Unyielding](../myrtle/).

Our current best image is the single-night RASA stack from November 12, 2026: **256 × 30 s**, **2 h 8 m**, Celestron RASA 8 + ASI2600MC Pro + Celestron Ha/Hβ/OIII.
