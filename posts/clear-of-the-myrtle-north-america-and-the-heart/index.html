Clear of the Myrtle: North America and the Heart

The plan was not subtle. The crepe myrtle in the northeast corner of the yard — Myrtle the Unyielding — still owns the Heart and Soul until they cross the meridian. The west was open. So on the night of November 12 we started with the North America Nebula, then swung to the Heart once it cleared the tree.

Same backyard near Mobile, same replacement RASA 8 at 400 mm f/2, ASI2600MC, Celestron Ha / Hβ / OIII filter from the RASA kit. The guide scope is still not mounted. Unguided 30-second subs at gain 100, on purpose, until the optics are fully tamed. That used to feel like a compromise. After this night, it feels like a method.

North America first, while the west was clear

The North America Nebula (NGC 7000) is a big, dim hydrogen cloud in Cygnus. At f/2 it does not stay dim for long.

More about NGC 7000, the North America Nebula →

256 of 270 frames kept, from 8:19 to 11:00 PM CST. 2 hours 8 minutes of integration. The Cygnus Wall — the bright ionization front that stands in for Mexico and Central America — is at lower left. This is the usual correct-parity, nearly north-up view: the dark lane to the right of the wall is the Gulf, the Pelican sits out in the Atlantic, and the continent reads the way it does on a map.

Two hours of 30-second subs from a light-polluted Gulf Coast yard. That is the RASA doing what we bought it to do.

Then the Heart, after the tree

November 5 we tried the Heart early and Myrtle won, the same way she won in October with the Askar. This time we waited until it transited, then ran until astronomical dawn.

633 of 646 frames kept, from 11:00 PM to 4:54 AM CST. 5 hours 16 minutes 30 seconds. The larger lobes sit on top, the point at the bottom, and the Fishhead (IC 1795) is tucked at lower right. This is the image the October 8-frame preview and the blocked November 5 run were waiting for. Those earlier sets are kept separate. They are not in this stack.

A 20-minute Askar record of the attempt still lives on the Heart’s target page. It can stay there as a reminder. This is the one that looks like a heart.

Where the color comes from

Calibration, registration, and integration ran in the morning without anyone having to start them by hand. Then GraXpert, BlurXTerminator, NoiseXTerminator, StarXTerminator, and a pass to neutralize star color so the nebula and the stars stop fighting.

Color on these two comes from spectrophotometric color calibration (SPCC), set up for the three passbands of the Celestron 93619 filter: hydrogen-alpha at 656.3 nm, oxygen III at 500.7 nm and hydrogen-beta at 486.1 nm, each about 12 nm wide. SPCC measures the stars in the frame against their catalogued spectra and balances the channels to match, so the color is measured, not picked. Hydrogen-alpha really is a deep red and oxygen III really is a blue-green. Your eye just can’t see it at the eyepiece, because the light is far too faint to trigger color vision. The camera, stacking hours of it, can.

The next job is in the corners

The centers of both fields are tight: about 2.9 to 3.4 pixels FWHM on the raw 30-second subs. Two of the corners stay close to that. The other two — top-left and bottom-right, as the camera shows them — open up to about 5 to 6 pixels and elongate along that diagonal. The same pattern shows up on both targets, all night. That is not tracking and it is not seeing. It is tilt, or the sensor not square to the optical axis, or collimation, and it is now a measured baseline rather than a vague complaint.

The pictures still hold. The next work is to tighten those corners.

What stuck

Myrtle does not get a vote after meridian. f/2 on a large emission target, even unguided, even from this yard, can fill a frame in one night. Color can be measured, even through a narrowband filter. And a pretty Heart was always going to depend more on scheduling than on wanting it badly enough in October.

North America is finished. The Heart is finished. The corners are the next project.

How it was made

  • Capture: Celestron RASA 8 (400 mm, f/2) + ZWO ASI2600MC Pro, Celestron Ha/Hβ/OIII (unit 93619), unguided 30 s at gain 100, backyard Gulf Coast near Mobile, night of November 12–13, 2026.
  • North America (NGC 7000): 256 of 270 × 30 s kept, 8:19–11:00 PM CST → 2 h 8 m.
  • Heart (IC 1805): 633 of 646 × 30 s kept, 11:00 PM–4:54 AM CST → 5 h 16 m 30 s. November 5 RASA frames and the October Askar preview were not combined.
  • Processing: calib / register / integrate (morning, unattended); GraXpert; BlurX / NoiseX / StarX; narrowband SPCC for the 93619 passbands (Hα 656.3, OIII 500.7, Hβ 486.1 nm, ~12 nm); star-color neutralization. North America is presented nearly north-up, east-left (lossless 90° rotation). The Heart is rotated for an upright presentation (lobes on top, point at the bottom; lossless 180° from the original finish), not north-up.
  • Notes: guide scope not mounted; corner star-shape tilt measured and left as the next optical job.
