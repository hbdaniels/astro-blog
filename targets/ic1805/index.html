The Heart sits about 7,500 light-years away in the Perseus Arm of our galaxy. William Herschel discovered it in 1787. The bright knot on its western edge was found first, so it got its own number, NGC 896.

What lights it up is a cluster of hot stars in the middle, some of them nearly 50 times the Sun’s mass. Their radiation is pushing the gas out into the heart shape.

It’s the brightest part of the “W4 superbubble,” a chimney that may carry hot gas from the galactic disk up into the halo. It’s next door to the Soul Nebula, and the pair are called the Heart and Soul. Ours is only a 20-minute preview so far, so this page is waiting for a real session.
