November, as promised
The November run is in. Once the Soul cleared Myrtle the Unyielding, Devourer of Photons, we got two nights with the RASA 8. The first, on November 13, clipped the eastern end. The second, on November 14–15, was recentered 33′ east: 558 × 30 s, 4 h 39 m, calibrated with a new set of flats. The October Askar preview is still in the archive. This page shows the recentered stack.

The Soul is also called the Embryo Nebula and, by radio astronomers, Westerhout 5. It’s usually named after the star cluster in its body, IC 1848, but several other small clusters are embedded in it too.

It sits right next to the Heart Nebula, and together they’re the “Heart and Soul,” part of a bigger complex of star-forming clouds.

Two galaxies, Maffei 1 and Maffei 2, sit in the same patch of sky behind it, but the Milky Way’s dust hides them so well that they’re very hard to see.

Our current best image is the v3 crop of the recentered RASA stack from November 14–15, 2026: 558 × 30 s, 4 h 39 m, Celestron RASA 8 + ASI2600MC Pro + Celestron Ha/Hβ/OIII, calibrated with the November 15 flats, shown in the camera’s own orientation, cropped to the nebula.
