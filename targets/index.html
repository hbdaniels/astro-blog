Targets

One page per object. Each one keeps our best current image, a short fact sheet, and every session where we’ve shot it, so it grows as we go back.

IC 1396 and the Elephant's Trunk

A huge glowing cloud in Cepheus with a dark, curling trunk of dust being eaten away by one star.

IC 1805, the Heart Nebula

A heart-shaped glow in Cassiopeia, powered by a cluster of very heavy stars.

IC 1848, the Soul Nebula

The Heart's neighbor, a nebula full of young star clusters.

IC 5070, the Pelican Nebula

The North America Nebula's neighbor, mid-transformation.

M31, the Andromeda Galaxy

The nearest big spiral galaxy, and one of the most distant things you can see with the naked eye.

M33, the Triangulum Galaxy

The third-largest galaxy in our Local Group, and a lot harder to see than its neighbor.

M42, the Orion Nebula

The closest big star factory to Earth, and bright enough to see without a telescope.

M45, the Pleiades

The Seven Sisters: a young star cluster drifting through a dust cloud that isn't its own.

Myrtle the Unyielding, Devourer of Photons

Not a deep-sky object. A tree. Messier's nemesis. (This is a joke page.)

NGC 1499, the California Nebula

A cloud of hydrogen shaped like California, and nearly impossible to see by eye.

NGC 7380, the Wizard Nebula

A young star cluster in Cepheus, discovered by Caroline Herschel.

The Eastern Veil Nebula

Part of the leftovers of a star that blew up thousands of years ago.

The Moon

Earth's only natural satellite, and the one target that never needs a dark sky.
