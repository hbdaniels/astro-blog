M42, the Orion Nebula

CatalogM42, NGC 1976
TypeEmission nebula (star-forming region)
ConstellationOrion
DistanceAbout 1,500 light-years
Magnitude4.0 (naked-eye)
Best seasonWinter; NASA says January is best, high in the south by mid-evening

M42 is about 1,500 light-years away, which makes it the closest large star-forming region to Earth. That’s why it shows up as a fuzzy “star” in Orion’s sword with nothing but your eyes.

The bright heart of it is the Trapezium, four massive young stars arranged in a trapezoid. All of them were born together right there, and their ultraviolet light is carving out the cavity that makes the glowing bowl in Hank’s image. The core is so much brighter than the outer wings that we blended in a set of short 10-second subs to keep it from burning out.

Hubble has found dusty disks around newborn stars inside it, the kind of disk our own solar system probably formed from 4.5 billion years ago. The Maya are said to have seen it as the cosmic fire of creation. That’s a pretty good description.
