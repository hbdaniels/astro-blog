NGC 1499, the California Nebula

CatalogNGC 1499, Sh2-220
TypeEmission nebula
ConstellationPerseus
DistanceAbout 1,000–1,300 light-years (estimates vary)
Apparent sizeAbout 2.5° long, five full Moons
Best seasonLate autumn into winter

It’s named for its outline in long exposures, which really does look like the state of California. By coincidence it also passes straight overhead in central California, because the latitude there matches the nebula’s declination.

It’s almost 2.5° long, too big for one frame on Hank’s setup, which is why it became a two-panel mosaic. Its surface brightness is so low that it’s extremely hard to see by eye, even through a telescope. A camera just has to sit on it for hours.

The glow comes from one hot, massive star, Xi Persei (Menkib). E. E. Barnard discovered it in 1884.
